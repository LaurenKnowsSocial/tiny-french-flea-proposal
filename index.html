What I see

A shop full of pieces people fall for on sight.

The trouble is timing. Someone has to be standing in front of the right piece at the right moment, and right now that is left to foot traffic and a bit of luck.

Good social changes who is in the room. It puts your pieces in front of the people already hunting for exactly them, so by the time something lands on the floor there is often already a name on it.

One honest thing first. Social is one lever, and it is the one I pull well. It will build your audience and move pieces, and it works best sitting alongside the rest of your marketing rather than carrying all of it on its own. I would rather say that now than let you expect social to do a job it was never built for.

A note on the bigger idea

On the other shops at Recollective.

You floated the possibility of opening this up so other shops could buy into my services. I am flattered you thought of me for it, and I want to be straight with you about why it is not the way I work.

I take on a small number of clients and go deep with each one, which is the whole reason the work is any good. Spreading myself thin across many accounts at a lighter touch would quietly undo the exact thing you would be paying for.

If Recollective's leadership ever wants to talk about social for the marketplace as a whole, one account and one voice for all sixty shops, I would be glad to have that conversation on its own. For now, this proposal is about your shop.
